
ADR Severity & Case Classification

1. Introduction

Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) classification is an important part of pharmacovigilance. It helps healthcare professionals evaluate the intensity, seriousness, expectedness, and clinical impact of suspected reactions to medicines.

2. Objectives

- To understand ADR severity and seriousness.
- To classify reactions as expected or unexpected.
- To assess the clinical impact of suspected ADRs.
- To understand the importance of pharmacovigilance reporting.

3. Severity Classification

Mild: Symptoms are usually temporary and require minimal intervention.

Moderate: Symptoms may require medical treatment, a change in medication, or additional monitoring.

Severe: Symptoms are intense or significantly affect the patient's condition and may require urgent treatment.

Severity describes the intensity of a reaction. It does not automatically determine whether the reaction is serious.

4. Seriousness Classification

An adverse reaction is considered serious if it results in any of the following:

- Death.
- A life-threatening condition.
- Inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of hospitalization.
- Persistent or significant disability or incapacity.
- A congenital anomaly or birth defect.
- Another medically important event requiring intervention to prevent one of these outcomes.

5. Expectedness Classification

Expected ADR: The reaction is consistent with the applicable approved product information.

Unexpected ADR: The reaction's nature or severity is inconsistent with the applicable approved product information.

Expectedness must be assessed against the appropriate product information, not simply whether the reaction seems familiar.

6. Hypothetical Case Studies

Case 1: Mild, Non-Serious, Expected

A fictional patient taking an antihistamine experiences mild drowsiness. The reaction is listed in the medicine's approved product information. No medical intervention is required.

- Severity: Mild
- Seriousness: Non-serious, based on the information provided
- Expectedness: Expected
- Clinical impact: Temporary discomfort; monitoring may be appropriate

Case 2: Moderate, Non-Serious, Expected

A fictional patient taking a medicine develops persistent nausea that requires consultation and supportive treatment but does not result in hospitalization or another seriousness criterion. Nausea is listed in the medicine's approved information.

- Severity: Moderate
- Seriousness: Non-serious, based on the information provided
- Expectedness: Expected
- Clinical impact: May affect adherence and require treatment review

Case 3: Severe and Serious, Unexpected

A fictional patient develops a life-threatening allergic reaction shortly after taking a medicine. Emergency treatment and hospitalization are required. For this example, assume the reaction is not described in the applicable approved product information.

- Severity: Severe
- Seriousness: Serious
- Expectedness: Unexpected, under the stated assumption
- Clinical impact: Immediate emergency care and urgent assessment are required

Case 4: Severe but Not Necessarily Serious

A fictional patient experiences intense itching that causes significant discomfort but does not meet a regulatory seriousness criterion. The reaction is documented in the approved medicine information.

- Severity: Severe
- Seriousness: Not necessarily serious; the full clinical circumstances must be assessed
- Expectedness: Expected
- Clinical impact: May require clinical evaluation and treatment

These are illustrative examples. Actual classifications depend on complete case details and the relevant product information.

7. Importance of ADR Classification

- Supports patient safety.
- Helps identify clinically important medicine-related risks.
- Improves the quality of pharmacovigilance reports.
- Supports appropriate clinical management.
- Helps identify unexpected reactions that may require further investigation.

8. Conclusion

Classifying ADRs by severity, seriousness, and expectedness helps healthcare professionals understand their clinical impact and document suspected medicine-related risks accurately. These categories are distinct and should be assessed separately.
